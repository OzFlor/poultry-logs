Flock behaviour, aggression and management

The flock continued to show severe aggression, with repeated interruptions to feeding and routine care.

- 07:42: The Garden Table rooster fought with David for a prolonged period. The caretaker could not check David for injuries at the time.
- 15:55: Feeding was interrupted approximately six to eight times because of fighting. During Bico Preto's feeding, objects were thrown from the garden table. The fight stopped after Bico's water was thrown. Branquinho was stressed. Some birds had minor facial bleeding.
- Some birds were isolated without their water and feed being checked. Some may have missed both. Isolated birds continued fighting with birds outside their enclosures.
- The caretaker estimates that four to six severe aggression episodes occur per year.
- The caretaker expressed that losing two or more roosters would be preferable to continuing with the present level of aggression and stress, if that resulted in better flock control.

20:07: The caretaker was unwell and had omitted some feeding and recording details. Egg measurements were not trusted and were cross-checked. Emergency isolation of roosters resulted in some birds being without feed or water. Some birds had low crops at sleeping time.

After dark, while handling Jóli, Branquinho and Bico Preto, the caretaker had to throw several fighting birds outside through the window. Birds involved included Beta, Gamy, Ventura, Red Neck and two others. Alpha was spared because of his leg injury. Red Neck chicks jumped outside to their mother. Red Neck, Gamy and Ventura were among the aggressive birds.

The caretaker suspects that a particular comb type may be associated with many of the most violent roosters in this flock. This remains a personal observation, not an established genetic or causal conclusion.
