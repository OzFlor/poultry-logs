## ENVIRONMENT

### Weather
- Retrospective observation: sunny throughout the day, occasionally weak sunlight; warm, not hot. Wind not observed systematically.

### Environmental / facility observations
- **09:05:** Large numbers of tiny mosquitoes at home, causing discomfort. Insect spray and burning products could not be used because Patrícia and two broody hens were inside.
- **16:38:** Two dove eggs observed on top of the garden fan. The fan was operating at maximum speed. The nest structure has very open mesh, approximately 3–5 mm, with a largely open underside. Concern whether airflow and heat conditions could affect incubation. Doves remain part of the flock environment, spending time indoors, eating and entering the house.
- A dead rat was found from overnight. Poisoning attempts have not resolved the rat population.

### Facility and supply constraints
- Isolation/quarantine demand has exceeded available capacity, interfering with normal flock operations and spatial management.
- Essential procurement remains urgent: praziquantel for parasite management and additional movable coops for isolated birds.
- Shopee Thailand presented persistent technical barriers: broken search functionality producing blank screens and Thai AI-chat messages that could not be copied, obstructing supplier communication.
- **19:07:** Identified immediate needs: hair clipper, movable chicken coops, praziquantel and a wall fan for the sleeping headquarters. Search access was blocked. A brief ordering window allowed purchase of one 2×2 m coop.

# BEHAVIOUR

**Flock context:** High-aggression environment.

- **09:45:** A Silkie hen picked feathers from Bico Preto, causing him to vocalise intensely. Cloth protection was placed around him.
- **10:10:** Gamma Phoenix violently attacked a Silkie hen inside the house. The attack was witnessed directly. Approximately four minutes were spent tracing/catching Gamma, causing major disturbance. Two Silkie roosters flew outside; isolated birds became highly stressed; the remaining flock ran, flew and vocalised intensely. Gamma cannot run normally because of a left-leg injury present since days old.
- **10:33:** Ventura was identified as the missing bird. He returned inside flying and vocalising loudly.
- **14:43:** Pequenina, after laying, was isolated following a fight with Pipi. The process triggered extensive running, screaming and flying. Alpha and Beta moved their movable coop approximately 20 cm during the disturbance. Branquinho vocalised but could not move. Pipi appeared lethargic in the movable coop. Release/management decisions remained difficult due to the aggression and care demands.
- **15:18:** Pipi was released. She walked poorly to the box to lay. The movable coop did not provide the usual stress-free laying conditions. This was considered an indicator of possible ill health. Pellets had been spilled, possibly during the earlier chaos, and were refilled.

**Caretaker concern:** Branquinho, Bico Preto, Jóli and possibly Pipi may be approaching terminal decline. This is a personal concern and not a prognosis.

### Retrospective observations / questions
Morning crop condition was confusing and concerning. Questions raised include possible worm blockage, whether the coccidiosis treatment administered two days earlier was inappropriate, possible failure of recent deworming, or possible excess ingestion of the dewormer. The chick had reportedly eaten a substantial amount of the collected dewormer group.

Hatched approximately 5–5.5 weeks earlier following two flood days. The first week was spent on flamed concrete; subsequent time involved garden restrictions. Concern about possible parasite exposure was raised, although the exposure period may have been limited.

The chick recovered substantially during the afternoon, slept with the family, appeared alert and did not avoid the caretaker. The observation was interpreted as possibly recognising the previous care.

These remain questions and observations, not confirmed diagnoses or causal conclusions.
