Manual Feeding a Gamecock with a Serious Neck Injury: How Food Moisture Affected Breathing

I have been manually feeding Bico Preto, a Gamecock with a serious neck injury. This has made feeding technically difficult, and I have noticed that both food texture and moisture can make a significant difference to his breathing during feeding.

An observation with raw rice

I normally prepare raw rice mixed with split mung beans and eggshell particles smaller than 2 mm. He likes this mixture, and I believe the eggshell particles may help with food breakdown.

However, I observed a particular problem when feeding raw rice in small pinches while his mouth was wet. The rice tended to stick to the surfaces inside his mouth.

This was followed by episodes of considerable breathing difficulty, heavy head shaking, an open beak and a strong red colour in his face, similar to red wine.

Adjusting the food according to mouth moisture

After he drinks voluntarily, or receives water by other means, I avoid offering raw rice until his mouth is sufficiently dry. I temporarily use pellets or other dry food instead.

My observations suggest that the combination of a wet mouth and raw rice can be particularly problematic for this bird.

An individual observation, not a feeding recommendation

This is my experience with one injured Gamecock. His neck injury makes manual feeding unusually complicated, and these observations should not be treated as a general feeding method for chickens.

The breathing episodes are concerning and may involve food interfering with normal breathing. They do not, by themselves, establish whether food entered his airway. A qualified avian veterinarian should assess recurrent breathing difficulty and the safest feeding approach.
