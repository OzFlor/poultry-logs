GameSilkies Protocol — Physical Measurements, Handling and Behavioural Trust

Author: José Flor – OzFlor
Project: GameSilkies, Fazenda Mondego
Protocol category: Physical development, morphology, measurement methodology and animal behaviour.

1. Purpose and Objectives

Physical measurements are collected to document individual development and morphological variation within the GameSilkies project.

The objectives are:

1. Establish individual physical measurement records for future comparison.
2. Document differences in leg dimensions between GameSilkies.
3. Monitor possible changes in leg proportions during development.
4. Preserve baseline measurements for comparison with future generations.
5. Investigate whether physical characteristics vary between individuals and gene-mother lines.
6. Build a longitudinal record without unnecessarily disturbing the birds.

These measurements are exploratory. They are intended to generate comparable observations, not to establish conclusions about bone density, muscle mass, skeletal strength or genetic inheritance without further evidence.

2. Lower-Leg Measurement Protocol

Measurement location

Measurements are taken at the approximate midpoint of the leg length.

The right leg is the standard reference leg.

Measurements collected

Two external dimensions are recorded:

- Front-to-back: external dimension measured in the front-to-back orientation.
- Side-to-side: external dimension measured perpendicular to the front-to-back orientation.

All measurements are recorded in millimetres (mm).

Current baseline measurements — 30 October 2026

Alpha Phoenix| 11.85 mm| 9.90 mm
Beta Phoenix| 9.80 mm| 8.54 mm

These are the first recorded comparative measurements under this protocol.

Measurement consistency

For future measurements:

- Use the same anatomical location.
- Maintain the same measurement orientations.
- Use the same unit of measurement.
- Record the date and time.
- Identify the individual and the leg measured.
- Preserve the original readings rather than replacing them with rounded or interpreted values.

Measurements should only be repeated when practical and without requiring unnecessary restraint.

3. Handling and Measurement Limitations

Measuring both legs is difficult and increases handling time.

Consequently, the project uses the right leg as its standard reference rather than requiring measurements of both legs.

The left leg is not routinely measured.

Lower-leg length measurements were considered but have been postponed.

The reason is deliberate: collecting additional physical data is not automatically more important than preserving the birds' confidence and voluntary interaction with the caretaker.

Measurements are supplementary to the project, not an objective that justifies unnecessary handling.

4. Behavioural Trust and Human Interaction

GameSilkies has an important behavioural objective: documenting and developing birds with docile, human-trusting characteristics.

The project therefore distinguishes between:

- A bird tolerating restraint.
- A bird remaining calm during unavoidable handling.
- A bird voluntarily approaching the caretaker.
- A bird accepting food and touch voluntarily.
- A bird choosing to remain close to humans without restraint.

Tolerance of handling should not automatically be interpreted as voluntary trust.

The caretaker aims to establish positive human interactions while avoiding unnecessary restraint and disturbance.

This is particularly important because the project intends to document behavioural characteristics alongside physical development.

5. Measurement Priority and Frequency

Physical measurements are collected when practical, particularly when the bird is already being handled for another necessary reason.

The following priorities apply:

Measurement or observation| Protocol
Body weight| Continue established individual weighing records
Right-leg dimensions| Occasional measurements when practical
Left-leg dimensions| Not routinely measured
Lower-leg length| Postponed
Spur dimensions| Optional; only when accessible without unnecessary restraint
Behavioural trust| Ongoing observation during normal interaction

There is no requirement to obtain every possible measurement or to maintain a rigid measurement schedule.

Missing measurements are intentionally left unrecorded rather than obtained at the expense of unnecessary handling.

6. Interpretation and Scientific Limitations

External leg dimensions represent the combined external structure of the measured area.

They do not independently establish:

- Bone density.
- Bone thickness.
- Muscle mass.
- Skeletal strength.
- Genetic inheritance.
- Sexual maturity.
- Overall physical fitness.

Differences between individuals should initially be described as observed morphological differences.

Repeated measurements may help document developmental changes, provided that anatomical location and measurement technique remain consistent.

Spur development is recorded separately from leg dimensions. Spur size and development may vary independently of external leg thickness and should not be treated as interchangeable characteristics.

7. Future Comparative Objectives

As the GameSilkies population develops, these measurements may become useful for:

- Comparing individual F1 birds.
- Comparing F1 individuals with future F2 offspring.
- Investigating variation between gene-mother lines.
- Documenting leg proportions in relation to body weight and age.
- Examining whether particular physical characteristics recur in subsequent generations.

Such comparisons must account for age, body size, measurement consistency and individual variation.

No conclusion about heritability should be drawn from the current three individuals alone.

8. Governing Principle

Behavioural trust takes priority over supplementary physical measurements.

The purpose of measurement is to understand and document the birds, not to make the birds submit to unnecessary procedures.

The project will collect physical information whenever practical while preserving voluntary interaction, confidence and docility as important characteristics in their own right.

9. Record Integrity

This protocol documents the methodology, reasoning and objectives behind the measurements.

Once published, it provides historical context for understanding:

- What was measured.
- Where measurements were taken.
- Which individuals were measured.
- Why the measurements were collected.
- Which measurements were deliberately omitted.
- What conclusions the measurements can and cannot support.

Future changes to methodology should be documented as subsequent protocol developments. Earlier observations and measurements remain historical records and should not be retrospectively rewritten.
