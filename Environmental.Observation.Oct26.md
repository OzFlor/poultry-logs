Environmental Observation Log — Nong Khae, October 2026

Wildlife, Insects, Floodwater, Drainage and Environmental Risks

Observer: José Flor — OzFlor
Location: Nong Khae, Thailand
Period: October 2026

1. Introduction

This record documents environmental conditions observed around my property in Nong Khae, including insects, wildlife, standing water, drainage, animals entering the property and potential risks to domestic poultry.

My objective is to maintain a longitudinal environmental record alongside my poultry and rainfall logs.

These observations are based on direct observation and repeated monitoring. Where I express a concern or interpretation, it is distinguished from something physically observed.

---

2. Floodwater and Drainage Conditions

Flooding has been a recurring environmental problem in this area.

During September and October, I repeatedly recorded standing floodwater, changes in water levels, drainage problems and differences between water levels at nearby drainage points.

On 2 October, two drainage points approximately 15 metres apart showed a water-level difference of approximately 315 mm.

I also documented suspected drainage obstructions and differences in water levels along the surrounding drainage system.

During the October flooding:

- On 8 October, floodwater exceeded the tops of my 36 cm gumboots in one area.
- Poultry had to be retrieved from flooded areas.
- Mud and standing water made access and bird recovery difficult.
- Areas occupied by pigeons remained flooded.

I have also previously recorded concerns about contamination of standing water, including possible sewage-related contamination and dirty drainage water.

These conditions deserve continued documentation because domestic poultry can come into contact with standing water, mud and organisms living in it.

I do not consider visibly clear water necessarily safe.

---

3. Insects and Organisms Associated With Floodwater

On 1 October, I observed thousands of extremely small flying insects positioned only centimetres above the floodwater.

They appeared to remain close to the water surface.

On 3 October, I conducted a more detailed inspection and observed:

- High concentrations of small insects near the end of Soi 8.
- Particularly high concentrations where dirty water accumulated.
- Small moving organisms in the water, possibly larvae or other aquatic organisms.

On 1 October, I also recorded substantial quantities of white bubbling material in the garden.

These observations are not sufficient to identify the organisms or determine their origin.

However, their repeated presence around standing water makes them relevant to environmental monitoring.

---

4. Mosquitoes, Flies and Ants

Insect populations vary considerably from day to day.

During October, I recorded:

- Days with almost no mosquitoes or flies.
- Sudden increases in mosquito populations.
- Mosquitoes around poultry sleeping areas.
- Small insects and ants around the property.
- Large variations in fly numbers.

On 27 October, I recorded significant numbers of small mosquitoes both inside and outside the house.

I have previously observed mosquitoes disturbing sleeping birds and biting them, as well as birds catching and eating mosquitoes.

I maintain regular cleaning of the garden, removal of food residues and washing of feeding containers.

Despite consistent cleaning, insect populations change substantially.

This suggests that other environmental factors may influence their abundance, although their causes have not been established.

---

5. Other Wildlife in the Garden

On 27 October, I recorded several wildlife observations that deserve continued monitoring.

Snails

Many snails of two different types were present in the garden.

I regularly remove them.

I have observed chickens consuming snails on the previous farm, but I have not established whether they consume the snails found at this location.

Frogs

Frogs are present in the garden.

One type was identified to me as potentially poisonous through skin contact. I have not independently identified the species.

Rats

Medium-sized rats were observed around the abandoned property at number 27, where pigeons and doves are also present.

I have observed rats entering my garden at night.

Mango seeds and other evidence indicate that they visit the property and may consume or transport food material.

Snakes

I have encountered several snakes in the garden.

During 2026, I killed four long snakes, measuring approximately 1.2 metres to nearly 2 metres, and one juvenile cobra.

The presence of snakes is an additional consideration when managing poultry and working around vegetation.

---

6. Rodent Control and Poultry Safety

Rodents entering the garden create concerns beyond food contamination.

They can damage equipment, enter unused coops and potentially expose poultry to disease or injury.

I have used poison pellets for rodent control and found three or four dead rodents.

However, this creates a separate concern:

Poultry could potentially encounter remaining poison pellets if they are not removed before the birds are released.

I have previously forgotten to remove remaining pellets before opening the coops.

This is an identified management risk that needs to remain documented.

The presence of dead rodents also raises questions about possible secondary exposure to other animals.

---

7. Dogs and Cats

The number of dogs regularly visiting the surrounding area has decreased substantially compared with earlier observations.

However, dogs continue to approach my gate.

Three cats have also been visiting the property.

I have previously recorded a Silkie rooster killed in an incident involving a neighbouring dog. Responsibility was disputed.

These observations are relevant because poultry are exposed to animals entering or approaching the property.

The presence of dogs and cats is therefore included as part of the ongoing predator and animal-interaction record.

---

8. General Environmental Management

My daily management includes:

- Regular cleaning of the garden.
- Removal of food residues.
- Washing feeding containers.
- Turning containers upside down when not in use.
- Removing snails.
- Monitoring mosquitoes and flies.
- Checking unused coops for rodent activity.
- Monitoring floodwater and drainage conditions.
- Observing wildlife entering the property.

The objective is to reduce unnecessary exposure of poultry to environmental hazards while maintaining a record of the organisms and conditions naturally present around the property.

---

9. Longitudinal Observation Objectives

This environmental record will help establish whether there are recurring relationships between:

1. Rainfall and standing water.
2. Flood duration and mosquito populations.
3. Dirty drainage water and aquatic organisms.
4. Flooding and wildlife movement.
5. Rodent activity and food availability.
6. Insect populations and poultry sleeping conditions.
7. Predator visits and poultry losses.
8. Seasonal changes in wildlife abundance.

These are monitoring objectives, not established causal relationships.

I intend to maintain continuity between observations rather than reconstructing environmental conditions from memory.

Original observation remains mine. AI assistance is limited to organization and normalization of the record.
