This organisation connects poultry veterinarians and avian health professionals from academia, research, government and private practice.

6. How I would present the archive to researchers


I would avoid presenting it simply as a poultry diary or a breeding project.


A more research-oriented description would be:


Independent Longitudinal Poultry Observation Archive




A continuously maintained collection of individual poultry records documenting growth, feeding, health observations, behaviour, welfare, environmental conditions and breeding histories.


The archive includes dated raw observations, AI-assisted normalized records, individual bird histories, weight measurements, photographs and video evidence. Records preserve chronology, source attribution and uncertainty, allowing subsequent review and interpretation.


The archive includes general poultry observations and a dedicated GameSilkies crossbreeding project. It is independently maintained by José António Flor de Sousa (OzFlor), with the objective of preserving detailed real-world poultry observations for transparency, future reference and potential research collaboration.




This wording does not claim scientific validation, but makes the archive's potential research value clear.


7. My suggested approach


I would approach researchers in this order:




PSA: Ask through your existing membership network for researchers interested in independent longitudinal poultry records.


WPSA: Contact its network for potential academic collaborators.


University research groups: Approach researchers working on poultry behaviour, welfare, nutrition and backyard poultry.


WVPA: Approach researchers interested in poultry health observations, while clearly distinguishing your observations from veterinary findings.




I would not initially send hundreds of logs. Instead, prepare a short archive introduction, a sample normalized log, a raw-log example and a description of your recording methods.


One important distinction: You are not asking researchers to endorse your conclusions. You are making original observations and supporting records available for independent examination.


That is a much stronger and more defensible proposition.


The next useful step would be to prepare a research collaboration introduction that presents the archive to universities and poultry science organisations without overstating its scientific status.
