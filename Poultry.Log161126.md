Interpretation notes

Jóli has a history of slow digestion, poor nutritional condition, passing hard plant material and feathers, and passing many elastic rubber bands during the year in this garden.

The previous day's cylindrical palpable structure was not found during the current examination. Several hard solids were palpated instead. Their identity remains unknown.

The observed proglottids and thin moving worms provide direct evidence of parasite-like material in the recorded stools. The proglottids are consistent with tapeworm segments, but species identification was not established. The very thin worms have not been identified.

The persistent yellow/green liquid, mucus and abnormal stool consistency remain observations; their exact origin and underlying cause are not established. The possibility of retained foreign material or obstruction remains relevant given the history, but is not confirmed by palpation or stool appearance.

Overnight stool observations

Two large stools were attributed to the mothers; other stools were considered too large to be from the chicks.

One fresh, wet stool showed fresh blood dispersed over the faecal mass, on top of white liquid. Certainty of fresh blood was high.

The caretaker considered several possible explanations:

- The group had received STOP-ONE dewormer approximately 4–5 days earlier.
- Blood had continued to be observed in the group through the current day.
- The two chicks received the first of two recorded days of coccidiosis treatment on the previous day.
- The blood appearance and timing were considered potentially consistent with a worm-related problem that the caretaker has been following for approximately two years.

However, the caretaker explicitly noted that no conclusion could be reached because findings are mixed. Earlier long-term blood attribution may have been incorrect, and this week's observations may also be misinterpreted due to overlapping digestive problems.

An additional feeding observation: when fresh corn and bread were served, the chicks ate rapidly and in substantial quantities. Their mothers also supplied additional food. This food was used to deliver the dewormer.

Both hens were sharing the maternity area.
