Poultry Environmental Risks: Pest Pressure, Poison Exposure and Predators

José Flor – OzFlor | Fazenda Mondego
Observation date: 27 October 2026

1. Purpose

This record documents the environmental conditions surrounding my poultry at the current location. It brings together observations of insects, ants, snails, frogs, rats, snakes, cats and dogs, together with the practical precautions and unresolved risks affecting poultry management.

These are observations from an operating poultry environment, not a controlled scientific study. Individual events and suspected causes must be distinguished from established conclusions.

2. Insects and Ants

On 27 October 2026, I observed:

- A few flies around the home.
- Significant numbers of small mosquitoes both inside and outside.
- Numerous tiny and larger ants in Area -1.
- Ants moving through the area where small poultry droppings were present.

I have been documenting flies, mosquitoes and ants over time. Their presence varies, and individual observations should be read alongside the dated environmental records rather than treated as a permanent condition.

My routine includes removing food from the garden at roosting time, washing food containers and placing them upside down on concrete to reduce fly access.

3. Rats and Food-Related Risks

Rats are present around an abandoned house and garden at number 27, where pigeons and doves are also present.

During the night, rats enter my garden and consume or carry mangoes. Five mango seeds were found in a coop that is not currently in use; I suspect the rats brought them there.

Rats previously made a hole in the Red Neck and Blue Neck enclosure. Gameegg1 fell through that opening and broke.

I have placed poison pellets in the garden and subsequently found three or four dead rats. The rats consume the poison occasionally.

The unresolved concern is secondary exposure of the poultry to the poison pellets themselves. On one occasion, I forgot to remove the remaining pellets before opening the coops. I am concerned that a chicken could eat a pellet if it remains accessible.

This is a genuine management concern, but I have not established that any chicken has consumed the poison.

4. Snails, Frogs and Snakes

The garden contains many snails of two types. My chickens ate snails at the previous farm, but I do not recall whether they have eaten them at this location. I continue to remove snails from the garden.

Frogs are also present, including a type I was advised not to touch because its skin may contain toxins. I have not independently identified the species.

Snakes enter the garden. During 2026, I killed four long snakes estimated at approximately 1.2 metres to nearly 2 metres, as well as one juvenile cobra.

These observations establish that several types of wildlife are present around the poultry area. They do not establish that every species has caused a poultry injury or loss.

5. Cats and Dogs: Direct Poultry-Safety Concerns

Cats and dogs remain a significant concern.

The local dog population has reportedly fallen to approximately one-third of its former level, but dogs still approach my gate. Three cats have recently been coming into the area.

One cat comes from the direction of the street behind numbers 26 and 27. At approximately 09:55 on 27 October, a large dark-yellow cat was standing on my fence with its head inside the property.

The three mother hens and four chicks were within approximately two metres of the cat. The birds sounded an alarm. When I screamed and ran toward them, the flock became highly vocal and ran around in panic. Bico Preto moved the movable coop and was on the concrete during the disturbance. One chick became separated, but I found it within seconds. The vocal response lasted approximately two minutes.

This incident documents a cat entering the birds' immediate surroundings and a strong flock alarm response. It does not prove that the cat intended to attack.

I have reported losing seven birds to cats and dogs on my property. One previous incident involved a Silkie rooster killed by dogs associated with a household whose owners refused responsibility, stating that their dogs could roam outside and that my chickens should remain inside.

I have also documented disputes and confrontational responses when attempting to address poultry losses. The details of individual incidents remain in the relevant earlier logs.

6. Routine Precautions and Remaining Problems

My ongoing precautions include:

- Removing food from the garden at roosting time.
- Cleaning and washing food containers.
- Placing cleaned containers upside down on concrete to reduce fly access.
- Removing snails from the garden.
- Monitoring insects, ants, rats and other wildlife.
- Observing poultry responses when cats or other potential predators approach.

The principal unresolved risks include rat access to poultry areas, possible access to poison pellets, openings in enclosures, wildlife in the garden, and cats or dogs entering the birds' surroundings.

The observations show why environmental management cannot be separated from daily poultry care. However, each suspected hazard should be documented without automatically attributing every loss, illness or behavioural response to it.
