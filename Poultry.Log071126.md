- Morning weight: 880 g ec.

Leg condition and activity

- 09:52: Left leg appeared slightly worse compared with the previous day.
- 17:09: Jumped from a tent approximately 2 m high for the third time observed that day. Landed with left-leg problems.

The caretaker compared this behaviour with a personal past experience of gliding and landing with a broken leg in a cast, using bushes to avoid another impact. The incident occurred in Algodres, approximately 6 km from the caretaker's town. "Kamikaze" was the nickname used at that time.

Evening flock disruption — caretaker's detailed observation

- 18:07 LT (logging time): Following Branquinho's isolation, the situation became highly chaotic and the caretaker lost control of the overall flock environment.
- Branquinho became very agitated and stressed, jumping, falling and moving across concrete surfaces in a manner described as resembling a snake.
- Pequenina followed by jumping and moving the movable coop, spilling water and pellets.
- At the same time, the caretaker was spending approximately 20 minutes providing dedicated care to Bico Preto and could not move without disrupting that session.

When the caretaker went to put Branquinho to sleep after dark:

- Branquinho attempted to attack the caretaker but could not stand.
- Once held, he produced considerable force and remained highly agitated.
- The caretaker had to hold him by both legs and both wings because other handling positions were not possible.
- He then moved in a manner described as resembling an accordion.
- Administering NVV was difficult.
- Once placed in his sleeping movable coop, he collapsed, became very quiet and did not move.
- Approximately five minutes later, the caretaker checked whether he could move. He did move, but very poorly.

Caretaker's broader interpretation of recurring flock episodes

The caretaker observed that most birds inside the sleeping areas became highly agitated, noisy and difficult to manage. Behaviours included mounting hens and jumping around.

The caretaker considered Branquinho's episode a possible trigger for the wider agitation.

Similar patterns were recalled from previous episodes involving Tóly, Tété and Tótó. The caretaker described these as recurring episodes in which one bird's behaviour appeared to trigger a cascade of agitation and aggression, sometimes involving hens as well as roosters. The caretaker also recalled previous cases in which birds suffered severe consequences, including death.

Caretaker's interpretation: Stress is considered one of the leading contributors to mortality in this flock, often appearing alongside other apparent causes. The caretaker considers stress a possible major contributor even when the immediate event is described as organ collapse.

Interpretation boundary: This is a longitudinal personal observation and causal interpretation. It is not a confirmed diagnosis or proof that stress caused any specific death or organ failure.
