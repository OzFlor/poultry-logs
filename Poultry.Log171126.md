Possible mating observation and F2 implications

- Close to sleeping time, when most birds were already roosting approximately one metre away from the caretaker, a mounting event was heard.
- Initially ignored, the event was then observed: a Silkie rooster without a left spur, who also regularly accompanies the caretaker at the garden table, was jumping off Beta's back.
- Beta was observed standing with her lower body against the surface, followed by body shaking and wing flapping after the event.
- The caretaker had previously noticed Beta searching boxes for a place to lay and interpreted this as possible laying behaviour.
- The only bird the caretaker had seen attempting to mount Beta was Gamma. A Silkie rooster had also made a remote attempt.
- The caretaker considers the observed event a serious potential disruption to the F2 breeding plan.
- A small movable garden coop, approximately 2 × 2m, is being considered to isolate Beta with her brother.
- The caretaker plans to investigate the duration of sperm retention in hens.

Reproductive clarification: Hens can retain viable sperm in specialised storage tubules in the reproductive tract. In chickens, fertile eggs may be produced for approximately 10–14 days on average, with fertility sometimes persisting for up to three weeks. Fertility generally declines after mating. Therefore, separation from a male does not immediately eliminate the possibility of fertilised eggs. The observed mounting does not establish successful insemination, and no fertilisation has been confirmed. "University of Maine Cooperative Extension" (https://extension.umaine.edu/publications/2072e/) and "Mississippi State University Extension" (https://extension.msstate.edu/publications/the-only-good-broiler-breeder-egg-fertilized-egg).
