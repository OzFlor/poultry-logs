# Spatial Grid Reference Protocol: The 10x4m Concrete Enclosure Mapping System
**Project:** GameSilkies Research Infrastructure
**Author:** José Flor – OzFlor | Fazenda Mondego
**Archive Reference:** GameSilkies.LogSpatialMapping | Published via @Fazenda.Mondego
### Introduction & Operational Rationale
In free-roaming, multi-tier backyard poultry environments, one of the greatest challenges for an independent researcher is biological attribution. When recording clinical indicators—such as parasite shedding, proglottid counts, or distinct stool morphologies—associating a random dropping with a specific sub-adult, broody hen, or recovering cockerel can introduce massive observational error.
To solve this without intrusive physical confinement, the GameSilkies research framework implements a standardized **Spatial Grid Reference System** across the primary concrete enclosure and tent footprint. This document details how the physical space is mapped, divided, and utilized to bridge raw observational logs with high-resolution epidemiological and behavioral tracking.
### Spatial Layout & Grid Architecture
The primary operational compound consists of a **10-meter by 4-meter concrete area**, partially sheltered under an **8x4-meter tent structure** originating from the North side.
To transform this open area into a precise coordinate matrix, the space is segmented as follows:
 1. **Macro Sectors (2\text{m} \times 2\text{m}):**
   The entire area is laid out in a grid of major sectors, measured systematically starting from the North-left boundary.
 2. **Micro Subsectors (1\text{m} \times 1\text{m}):**
   Each 2\text{m} \times 2\text{m} macro sector is further divided into four distinct 1\text{m} \times 1\text{m} subsectors, designated alphabetically from North-left to South-right:
   * **A** (North-West quadrant)
   * **B** (North-East quadrant)
   * **C** (South-West quadrant)
   * **D** (South-East quadrant)
*Example notation from field logs:* Sector 8D or Sector 4D points directly to a precise 1\text{m} \times 1\text{m} square within the matrix where clinical samples (e.g., specific proglottid counts or stool types) were physically recovered.
### Why Spatial Grid Mapping Matters for Field Research
By implementing a Cartesian-style coordinate system on a backyard poultry layout, the manual logging framework achieves several advanced data-science objectives:
 * **Behavioral & Territorial Clustering:** Different cohorts and individual birds (such as the F1 Phoenix line, broody hens, or isolated cockerels) exhibit distinct resting and movement habits. Over time, logging drops by coordinates (8D, 4D) exposes natural heatmaps of where specific birds congregate, roost, or transit.
 * **Environmental Risk & Parasite Hotspots:** Cestodes (tapeworms) rely on intermediate hosts like ants, beetles, and snails that thrive in specific micro-climates (damp spots, edges near garden beds, or areas under feeding trays). Grid tracking allows the identification of high-risk subsectors where reinfection loops are most active.
 * **Compatibility with Automated & AI Frameworks:** Modern computer vision and smart agricultural systems rely entirely on pixel matrices and spatial bounding boxes to analyze animal behavior. Human-logged grid coordinates provide a structured, machine-readable dataset that mirrors digital mapping logic, making longitudinal field data far more valuable for comparative analytics.
