# 10. CARETAKER RECORDING METHOD AND LIMITATIONS

The author describes the morning weighing procedure:

* In low light, bedroom, bathroom, and kitchen lights are combined, and doors are opened or closed to control illumination and keep birds calm.
* Birds are picked up one by one, weighed, and their weights written on paper.
* Doors may need to be opened further to obtain sufficient light for calculations.
* Paper notes are moved to a brighter location to calculate weight changes and decide whether immediate boost feeding or isolation is needed.
* Some birds cannot be identified confidently under dim lighting, requiring the use of a phone light.
* During weighing, the author also monitors aggression and obtains feed or places birds in secure areas for individual feeding.
* The process takes approximately 20 minutes and may take longer for birds such as Branquinho and Pipi, which are difficult to place on the scale.

The author notes that quick calculations are important for care decisions but that reading or arithmetic errors can occur under these conditions. The record therefore preserves the original observations and makes calculations explicit rather than silently altering raw values.

### Drainage

The rainwater drainers remained blocked. The author reports images from the previous day showing a drainer filled to the top of its concrete base. The garden contains a hole where the underground water level is below the drainer.

### Clarification of the author's recordkeeping approach

The author objects to interpretations that incorrectly present observational logs as medical advice. The author states that urgent actions may be empirical, based on experience or trial and error when the cause is not known.

The distinction is important: **recording an action and its subsequent outcome is not the same as claiming a diagnosis or recommending treatment to others.** Statements of uncertainty are part of the record's integrity.
