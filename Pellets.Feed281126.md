1. Purpose of this record

This entry documents the identification and available product information for the pelleted feed used in my poultry flock. It establishes a reference point for future interpretation of feeding records, weight changes, growth, laying activity, stool observations and general flock development.

The purpose is not to evaluate the feed's nutritional adequacy or establish a diagnosis. It is to preserve the known product information and clearly identify nutritional values that remain unknown.

The available label information does not establish the complete nutritional composition.

In particular, the following values have not yet been confirmed:

- Sodium and chloride concentrations
- Vitamin and trace-mineral concentrations
- Amino-acid profile and digestibility
- Metabolisable energy

These missing values are relevant when interpreting the feed's suitability for birds at different developmental and reproductive stages.

4. Feeding context in my flock

The flock is mixed in age, size and reproductive status. It includes:

- Adult laying and non-laying hens
- Adult roosters
- Juvenile gamecocks
- GameSilkies hybrids at different developmental stages
- Silkie and other small-bodied poultry

The birds do not all have the same nutritional requirements. A layer ration intended for actively laying hens should not automatically be assumed to be nutritionally appropriate for growing juveniles or non-laying birds.

Accu-Lay 6630 is part of the flock's documented feeding context. Future analysis must consider which individuals consumed it, the quantity available, other foods consumed and their developmental or reproductive status.

5. Additional food sources: chicken skeletons and eggshells

Chicken skeletons are provided as a multipurpose food source, not exclusively as a calcium supplement.

The material can provide:

- Dark meat and associated nutrients
- Protein
- Bone tissue and its mineral content
- Marrow and other residual tissues
- Other nutrients depending on the amount of meat and tissue remaining

The actual nutritional contribution varies with the skeleton's condition, the amount of meat remaining, preparation and how much each bird consumes. It should not be treated as a measured or uniform calcium dose.

Eggshells are also present in the poultry environment. Over time, I have spread eggshell material over the soil. In addition, eggshell pieces smaller than approximately 3 mm have sometimes been mixed with the pellets, making them available for consumption.

These sources are recorded because they may influence total mineral intake. Their individual contributions have not been quantified.

6. Calcium and phosphorus: unresolved questions

The presence of oyster shell, dicalcium phosphate and/or calcium carbonate in the commercial feed indicates that calcium-containing mineral ingredients are included in its formulation.

However, the actual calcium concentration remains unknown from the available guaranteed analysis.

The same applies to phosphorus. Dicalcium phosphate is a source of both calcium and phosphorus, but its presence does not establish the total phosphorus concentration, available phosphorus level or final calcium-to-phosphorus ratio.

The additional eggshell and chicken skeletons introduce further variability.

Consequently, it is not currently possible to calculate the flock's total daily calcium or phosphorus intake, or determine whether any individual bird receives too much or too little.

This uncertainty is particularly important when comparing actively laying hens with growing birds and non-laying adults.

7. Future analysis and interpretation

This product identification should be considered alongside individual poultry logs, rather than as an isolated nutritional record.

Potential future comparisons include:

- Feed consumption and individual body-weight changes
- Growth patterns in juvenile GameSilkies and gamecocks
- Egg production and eggshell observations
- Feeding preferences and food acceptance
- Stool observations and changes over time
- Differences between laying and non-laying birds
- The contribution of supplementary foods to the overall diet
- Possible associations between feeding patterns and health observations

Any association identified through these records must remain an observation or hypothesis unless supported by appropriate nutritional analysis or other evidence.

8. Documentation limitations

This entry is based on the product identity and label information available at the time of documentation. It does not include laboratory analysis of the feed or measurements of the supplementary foods.

The exact calcium, phosphorus, energy and micronutrient concentrations remain unverified. Ingredient alternatives may also mean that the formulation varies between batches.

The registration number and formula code are retained as identification references for future product verification.

Record status: Product identified; nutritional composition partially documented; mineral concentrations unresolved.

Interpretation principle: Preserve the feeding history and observed outcomes without inventing missing nutritional values or assuming causation.
