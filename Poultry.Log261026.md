8. BRANQUINHO

Systematic neurological problems; weighing discontinued because of neurological issues.

8.1 Overnight entrapment and rescue

Incident date: 25 October 2026, approximately 22:30 LT.

Disappearance and discovery

- I could not find Branquinho in his usual sleeping area or outside despite a thorough search.
- I found him wedged behind a heavy mat or carpet leaning against a door and wall, directly behind Bico Preto’s dome-shaped coop.
- He was trapped upside down in an extremely narrow space, with a gap of approximately 15 cm.
- He was completely unable to free himself.

Condition immediately after rescue

- After removal, he was unsteady and walked while falling forward.
- I placed him in a movable coop with a carpeted floor.
- I administered one RAINBOW tablet, recorded as a vitamin B-complex supplement.

Morning-after observations

- By morning, Branquinho appeared alert and was crowing.
- After coop opening, he was kept isolated on the concrete area.
- His stools appeared normal.
- He was eating voluntarily and reasonably well, although he refused pellets.

These are observations of his condition following rescue; they do not establish why his condition improved.

8.2 Possible explanation for the entrapment

The following is my reconstruction of how the incident may have occurred, not a directly witnessed sequence.

- Physical setup: A narrow gap had inadvertently formed between a heavy, rigid, textured mat and a vertical wooden door propped against the wall behind the pink dome coop.
- Possible entry: Given his existing neurological and balance problems, Branquinho may have wandered behind the coop, lost his footing and slid into the gap.
- Possible upside-down position: The narrow space may have prevented him from using his wings or legs to stabilise himself.
- Possible post-rescue instability: Restricted movement, an awkward posture, fatigue or muscle stiffness, combined with his existing neurological condition, may have contributed to the forward-tipping gait observed immediately after rescue.

These explanations remain hypotheses. The precise movements, duration of entrapment and physiological effects were not witnessed or measured.
