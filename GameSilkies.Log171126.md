Possible mating event — approximately 17 November, shortly before roosting

The caretaker heard mounting activity and subsequently observed a Silkie rooster, identified by the absence of a left spur, jumping off Beta's back. Beta was standing with her lower body on the surface and was shaking her body and flapping her wings.

The caretaker had previously observed:

- Gamma Phoenix attempting to mount Beta.
- A more distant or incomplete mounting attempt by a Silkie rooster.
- Beta searching boxes, which the caretaker interpreted as possible pre-laying behaviour.

The newly observed event is stronger evidence of a mating attempt or mating than the earlier observations. However, the observation does not establish successful insemination, fertilisation, or the identity of sperm present in any future fertile egg.

The caretaker considered this an unintended event that could compromise the planned F2 breeding direction. The identity of the observed Silkie rooster should be recorded as the likely male involved, but the event does not establish paternity.

Sperm storage and possible fertility window

Hens can store sperm in specialised sperm-storage tubules in the oviduct after mating. In domestic chickens, sperm may remain capable of fertilisation for approximately 2–3 weeks, although fertility is not constant throughout that period. The highest fertility following a single mating is generally concentrated in the first few days.

Consequently:

- Eggs laid after this event may be fertile even if Beta is separated from the rooster.
- Separating Beta from all males prevents further mating but does not immediately eliminate sperm already stored.
- A future fertile egg cannot be assumed to have been fertilised by Gamma or by the Silkie rooster without further evidence.
- If the breeding objective is to establish parentage, eggs should be individually dated and tracked, with the possible mating event recorded as a parentage uncertainty.

The caretaker planned to find a small movable enclosure, approximately 2 × 2m, to separate Beta with her brother. This would prevent further uncontrolled mating, but would not resolve the existing sperm-storage uncertainty.
