2.2 Environmental Pests, Wildlife and Predator Risks

08:18 LT — Environmental observations

- A few flies were present around the home.
- Small mosquitoes were numerous both inside and outside.
- Area -1 had a significant number of the usual tiny and larger ants.
- The garden contains numerous snails of two types. Chickens ate snails at the previous farm; whether they consume them at this location has not been established.
- Frogs are present, including a type the caretaker was advised not to touch because of potentially poisonous skin secretions.
- Medium-sized rats are present around the abandoned house and garden at number 27, where pigeons and doves are also present.
- Rats enter the garden at night and consume or carry mangoes there. Five mango seeds were found in an unused coop; the caretaker suspects the rats brought them there.
- Rats previously made a hole in the Red Neck and Blue Neck enclosure, through which Gameegg1 fell and broke.
- Poison pellets have been placed in the garden. Three or four dead rats were found afterward. The rats consume the poison occasionally.
- Unresolved safety concern: The caretaker is concerned that chickens might access poison pellets if any remain when the coops are opened. On one occasion, remaining pellets were not removed before opening the coops.
- Snakes enter the garden. Four long snakes, estimated at approximately 1.2 m to nearly 2 m, and one juvenile cobra were killed during the current year.
- The caretaker regularly removes snails from the garden.
- Food is removed from the garden at roosting time. Food containers are cleaned and washed, then placed upside down on concrete to reduce fly access.

Flies, mosquitoes and ants have been logged previously. Dogs and cats have also been repeatedly documented as environmental and poultry-safety concerns.

The dog population around the property has reportedly fallen to approximately one-third of its former level, but dogs still approach the gate. Three cats have recently been coming into the area. One cat comes from the direction of number 23, where the caretaker previously lost a Silkie rooster described as a shoulder rooster to dogs belonging to that household. The owners refused responsibility, arguing that their dogs could roam outside and that the chickens should remain inside.

The caretaker reports having lost seven birds to cats and dogs on the property. Previous logs contain additional details about these incidents and disputes over responsibility.

These observations describe the local environment and the caretaker's reported experiences. They do not establish that every pest, wild animal or predator caused a particular poultry loss.

6. BEHAVIOUR

6.1 Aggression, Alarm and Flock Response

Context: This is a high-aggression flock environment.

Approximately 09:55 — Cat approaching the fence

The three mother hens and four chicks were on the southwest side of the garden, within approximately 2 m of a large, dark-yellow cat standing on the fence. The birds sounded an alarm. The caretaker was inside and saw the cat on the fence, with its head inside the property.

The cat occasionally comes from another street behind numbers 26 and 27.

The caretaker screamed and ran toward the birds. The flock became highly vocal and ran around in panic. Bico Preto moved the movable coop and was on the concrete during the disturbance. One chick became separated from the others, but the caretaker found it within seconds.

The vocal response lasted approximately two minutes.

This entry records the observed sequence. It does not establish that every bird responded identically or that the cat intended to attack.
