# 8. General Flock and Caretaker Observations

## Weighing routine and measurement limitations

The caretaker weighs individual birds while it is still dark, using a combination of bedroom, bathroom and kitchen lighting. Doors are opened or closed as needed to provide sufficient light while keeping birds calm and preventing them from moving freely around the home.

Birds are picked up individually, weighed and recorded on paper. The caretaker calculates weight changes promptly because the results may influence immediate decisions about supportive feeding or isolation.

Additional light may be obtained by opening a door slightly and moving the written records toward a light source. A phone light is sometimes used when birds cannot be identified in dim conditions.

The process is complicated by simultaneous responsibilities, including controlling aggressive behaviour, obtaining food, preparing yolk mixture and moving individual birds to secure feeding locations away from the flock.

The routine generally takes approximately 20 minutes or longer. Branquinho and Pipi are particularly difficult to place on the scale.

The caretaker acknowledges that poor lighting, fatigue and multitasking can result in misreading figures or making calculation errors. The figures are checked again later when possible.

**Interpretation:** Initial measurements and calculations are working records, not automatically verified final values. Subsequent checking is part of the recordkeeping process. Corrections should be explicit, and uncertainty should be retained where the original information cannot be confirmed.

## Interpretation and documentation integrity

At **14:52 LT**, the caretaker noted concern that AI-generated interpretations of the logs sometimes present medical cautions in a way that fails to acknowledge the integrity of the underlying observations.

The caretaker does not claim to provide veterinary diagnoses. When urgent care is necessary and the cause is unknown, actions may be based on practical experience, empirical observation or trial and error.

An explicit statement of uncertainty means that the cause is not known; it should not be rewritten as an established diagnosis.

For example, the Branquinho record documents worm-like material observed following praziquantel treatment, persistent bleeding, subsequent coccidiosis treatment and the reported cessation of bleeding on day 3 or 4. These events are part of the historical record. The sequence does not by itself prove whether coccidiosis, worms or multiple conditions were responsible.

The record should preserve both the caretaker's observations and the limits of what can be concluded from them.
