### Untrusted Thai products

I don't trust products in Thailand. Those pellets have a lot of missing high important information. The same goes with most medication products here.

Your skepticism makes complete sense—especially when you want exact, transparent data rather than broad estimates.
There are a few major structural reasons why Thai feed bags and local veterinary or human medications look so sparse compared to what you would see on labels in places like Australia or the EU:
1. Why the Feed Bag Omits Exact Ratios and Values
 * Least-Cost Formulation & "And/Or" Groupings: Under Thailand’s Department of Livestock Development (DLD) labeling laws (พระราชบัญญัติควบคุมคุณภาพอาหารสัตว์), manufacturers are allowed to register a single formula license (like 01 02 34 0611) using broad "และหรือ" ("and/or") ingredient categories. This lets the mill swap corn for cassava, or soybean meal for peanut meal, week to week based on commodity prices without reprinting bags or re-registering the license.
 * No Exact Calcium, Phosphorus, or Vitamin Breakdown: Thai law only requires printing four basic minimums/maximums on standard commercial bags: crude protein, crude fat, crude fiber, and moisture. Even though 6630 is a layer feed containing ground shell, dicalcium phosphate, and calcium carbonate, the exact percentages of calcium, available phosphorus, sodium, methionine/lysine, or exact IU levels of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) are kept as proprietary mill specs rather than printed on the sack.
 * No Batch-Specific COA on Retail Bags: Unlike laboratory or specialty feeds that include a batch Certificate of Analysis, standard agricultural bags rarely show mycotoxin test results, exact ash content, or specific preservative compounds (printed simply as สารถนอมคุณภาพอาหารสัตว์ / "feed preservatives").
2. Why Medications Here Have the Same Issue
 * Minimalist Box & Bottle Labeling: Many local veterinary products, herbal blends, and even human OTC medications sold at local pharmacies or agro-shops list only the primary active ingredient (or just a broad category) while omitting full excipient lists, carrier oils, preservatives, or detailed pharmacokinetic/contraindication inserts.
 * Repackaging & Loose Sales: Agro-supply shops frequently break down bulk sacks of feed or bulk containers of supplements/medications into unlabelled clear plastic bags or small bottles, separating the product completely from its batch number, storage instructions, and expiration date.
Practical Ways to Work Around the Missing Information
When labels leave critical variables unknown, the safest ways to maintain control over what goes into your birds—and what you record in your logs—usually come down to:
 * Checking the Stamped Batch & Expiry Date: On sacks like the 6630, the bottom line says วันผลิต/วันล่วงอายุ (manufacture/expiry date), which is usually ink-stamped along the bottom seam or top stitching—always worth verifying before opening, since high heat and humidity degrade vitamins and encourage mold rapidly.
 * Keeping Base Feed Simple or Supplementing Manually: Because you cannot see the exact vitamin, mineral, or fat-soluble levels inside commercial pellets, many careful keepers treat commercial pellets strictly as a base calorie/protein ration and rely on known whole foods or single-ingredient supplements where the exact dose and composition are transparent.
 * Looking Up DLD / Thai FDA Registration Numbers: For medications or supplements that do print a Thai FDA (อย.) or DLD registration number, you can sometimes cross-check the registered active ingredients in the government database, even when the physical packaging leaves details off.
