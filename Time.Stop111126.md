### Repeated “Time-Stop” Behaviour — Pipi and Pequenina
**Retrospective entry: 12 November 2026**  
**Observation date: 11 November 2026 and two previous occurrences**

Three similar episodes have now been observed involving Pipi and Pequenina, consistently following Tchétché's intervention.

#### 1. Latest episode — 11 November 2026

Pipi was tied near the wall with approximately 1.2 m of rope. The knot around her leg was loose enough to accommodate two pens between the knot and her leg. She had approximately 180° of available movement, with food and water within reach.

Tchétché repeatedly picked at Pipi's face. This appeared demonstrative rather than an attempt to cause injury. I moved Tchétché away, but Pipi remained in the same position.

When I tried to encourage Pipi to move away, she moved only a few centimetres. She remained in this state for approximately six minutes, until I eventually made her move away.

#### 2. Previous two episodes

On two earlier occasions, both Pipi and Pequenina displayed similar behaviour after Tchétché had acted towards them.

On those occasions, both birds were tied in the middle of the garden, each with approximately 1.2 m of rope and 360° of available movement. Food and water were accessible.

The same type of intervention from Tchétché preceded both episodes.

#### 3. Behaviour after the episodes

When the birds eventually move, they move very slowly, with little apparent motivation. Their attention appears unfocused, giving them an unusual appearance resembling a trance or hypnotic state.

This is a description of their observed behaviour, not a diagnosis.

#### 4. Tchétché's social behaviour

Tchétché is the older female leader of the group. She becomes very upset when other birds fight and sometimes attempts to intervene, although her age makes her movements slow.

She frequently uses crows to express disapproval and communicate that certain behaviour is not tolerated. Her vocalisation can resemble a male's crow.

Her social authority does not appear to depend on physically hurting other birds. Head movements, low vocalisations and her presence alone are often sufficient to make other birds keep their distance. This is particularly noticeable when food is involved: the others may move away and allow Tchétché to eat pea flowers or other food without direct physical aggression.

Tchétché has consistently demonstrated intelligent and calculated social behaviour.

#### 5. Preliminary interpretation

The three episodes share a consistent preceding event: Tchétché's intervention.

This makes her presence and disciplinary behaviour the strongest identified common trigger in the observations so far.

One possible interpretation is a prolonged defensive or socially induced behavioural inhibition in Pipi and Pequenina. The particularly interesting feature is that the behaviour persists after Tchétché has been moved away. The birds do not immediately return to normal activity, and their subsequent movement appears unusually slow and unfocused.

I am provisionally describing this phenomenon as **“time-stop behaviour”**. This is a descriptive term for the observed episodes, not an established biological or veterinary diagnosis.

The observations do not yet establish the underlying mechanism. Further occurrences may help clarify whether this is a prolonged fear response, social submission, defensive immobility or another form of behavioural inhibition.

**Recording note:** The three episodes should remain separate observations, with their individual circumstances preserved. The repeated association with Tchétché is an emerging pattern, not proof of a specific physiological mechanism.
