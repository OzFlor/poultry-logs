GENERAL OBSERVATIONS

Environmental contamination and attempted foreign-object ingestion

Companheira — 07:24

Companheira attempted to swallow a long, worm-like object in the contaminated garden area.

A similar incident occurred on the previous day. During that earlier event, she attempted to swallow discarded material from the same area. I intervened, but approximately half of the unidentified object was swallowed before I managed to retrieve the remainder. I did not see or identify the object.

There is accumulated rubbish, including car parts and other debris, around the water tap and water tank near a mango tree. In this garden, accumulated rubbish around different trees reaches approximately 10–40cm in height.

On 13 November, while trying to prevent another possible ingestion, I rushed towards Companheira and caught her by the tip of her left wing. She screamed repeatedly and lost several feathers.

The intervention caused substantial short-term disturbance:

- Nearby birds ran or flew away.
- Birds in isolation jumped inside their movable coop.
- Bico Preto became very vocal, as did several other birds.

The object was not identified. Its worm-like appearance does not establish that it was a worm.

Area conditions: The ground is dry, hard clay, with construction debris including concrete, wall blocks and fibre-cement material. I do not consider this area suitable worm habitat.

Companheira — 09:28

Companheira found another discarded object and attempted to eat it.

This time, the object was identified as a rubber band with a knot (photograph recorded at 09:28:15).

She attempted to take it but did not ingest it. I used a small movable coop to catch and contain her before ingestion.

Several other birds followed her and attempted to steal the rubber band. This caused considerable chasing and flock disturbance. The rubber band was recovered before ingestion.

Outcome: No ingestion was observed. Companheira was caught using the small movable coop. The incident caused substantial short-term disturbance among several birds.

Environmental relevance: The identified rubber band confirms that discarded rubber material is accessible in the same contaminated garden area where Companheira previously attempted to swallow an unidentified worm-like object. Accessible rubbish and foreign materials remain an ingestion risk.

Garden ground conditions

Approximately 25% of the garden has received topsoil. An additional layer of approximately 20cm is needed across the remaining areas to reduce contamination risks and improve protection against recurring floods.

Flooding occurs multiple times a year in this garden, with the worst events lasting approximately two weeks. Financial limitations currently prevent completing the topsoil work.

At 10:04, I recorded observations of how rubbish is sometimes accumulated around trees. In some places, the material is later arranged into low surrounding walls or seating structures, occasionally with a slab above and an opening around the tree.
