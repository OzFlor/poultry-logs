Environmental observations and pest management

Mosquitoes and ventilation — evening of 4 November

- After 19:00, while washing Bico Preto's bedding outside, I noticed numerous small mosquitoes.
- I consequently closed the access doors to the outdoor sleeping coops and the windows of the indoor sleeping areas.
- Although I did not observe mosquitoes inside, I considered that some might be present.
- Closing the doors is not ideal because of heat accumulation. Closing the windows is also undesirable because gases may accumulate overnight.
- Two fans were running inside the indoor sleeping areas.

Ants

- Tiny ants were observed in the indoor sleeping areas, particularly Areas -1 and 0, their usual locations.

Rat observations and removal

- Approximately 12:35: Blue Red Neck sounded an alarm while standing in Sector 7, looking towards Sector 10d and part of the southwest garden.
- This is an area where I previously killed two snakes and missed another. My attention was initially focused on the possibility of another snake.
- I was holding Bico Preto and could not easily pick up a wooden stick.
- Upon approaching, I observed a young but relatively large rat emerging from behind a food container and jumping towards Blue Red Neck, Gamy, the two chicks and other birds.
- The birds were staring at the rat, but only Blue Red Neck sounded the alarm.
- I could not intervene immediately while holding Bico Preto.
- That morning, only two grains of rat poison remained underneath the garden table.
- 14:52: Blue Red Neck spotted the rat again underneath the outdoor sleeping coops.
- I decided to place the final dose of the available poison that evening, expecting another supply the following day.
- Approximately 15:00: Blue Red Neck alerted again. I killed the rat. Images were taken.
- The rat appeared thin and very weak, possibly after consuming the poison and continuing to search for food.
- I felt pity for it, but considered its removal necessary to protect the flock.
- It was buried at number 26 to prevent flies accumulating around the carcass.

7.1 Housing, flooding and local conditions

11:01 — Continuing dispute concerning poultry housing and responsibility for flooding

This subject has remained unresolved for approximately three months.

I continue to question why I am being pressured to construct a separate chicken coop, described in my own words as a Thai-style, third-world-type structure, when the house itself functions as the birds' housing.

I also question why my chickens are being accused of contaminating the local floods over the preceding two weeks, when local residents caused the drainage blockage and were aware that it was creating multiple problems for me and my birds.

A local couple previously accompanied me while I checked and measured the drainage channels. They observed that, over a distance of less than 15 metres on a flat surface, the water-level difference exceeded 300 mm. They subsequently told me that water behaves this way in Thailand and that it is normal.

Record distinction: These are my observations, questions and account of the discussions. The drainage measurements and statements are recorded as reported, without independent verification of the explanations given.
