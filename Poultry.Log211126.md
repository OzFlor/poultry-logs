Feeding and clinical observations

- Large quantities of yellow mucus, with an egg-white-like appearance.

During handling, Jóli appeared lethargic. Contact with the abdomen caused her to retract both legs. She appeared to make considerable efforts to expel retained material, but nothing came out. The user suspected she was trying to expel foreign material that had reportedly remained inside for more than three weeks.

The user massaged the abdomen during this episode. No material was expelled.

- 09:15: Sitting with beak lowered near the surface, tail bobbing, eyes open.

13:40 — Further intervention and historical reproductive context

The user checked Jóli again. She appeared to experience abdominal pain and vocalized with a sound described as air-like, but not originating from the air.

Relevant historical context: When Jóli began laying, reproductive organs had previously protruded externally. The user spent weeks cleaning and gently repositioning the tissue, followed by many months of cleaning and drying while liquid continued to emerge. During that period she drank constantly. She subsequently resumed laying normally and incubated eggs on at least two occasions. She has not laid for more than one year.

At 13:40, the user described her abdomen as severely concerning and observed repeated efforts to expel something.

Before the internal examination, dried faecal material accumulated on the vent feathers was washed away. The material appeared to have been present for a prolonged period and contained considerable moisture.

The user wet a small finger with oil and slowly inserted it into the vent. A large circumferential structure was felt, with a deeper tubular structure below it. The identity and anatomical location of the structure remain undetermined. The user asked whether it could be an egg; this has not been established.

Interventions recorded:

- 1.6 ml CCO coconut oil administered orally.
- 1 ml fry oil applied downwards at the vent. The exact extent of external versus internal application was not established.

Safety note: The structure has not been identified. No further internal examination, probing, massage or oil administration is recommended. The observation should not be treated as confirmation of an egg or obstruction.

Clinical interpretation — provisional: The record contains signs involving both gastrointestinal function and the abdomen/reproductive region. Persistent abnormal crop contents, declining recorded weights, abnormal stools, collapse, abdominal discomfort and unsuccessful straining are serious concerns. The previous reproductive prolapse and prolonged discharge make reproductive-tract disease another relevant possibility. The structure felt internally cannot be identified from the available observation or photographs. No diagnosis is established.

The user's reported absence of veterinary access is acknowledged. Further internal manipulation and forced feeding while Jóli is weak or showing abnormal breathing should be avoided. Quiet, clean accommodation and observation of voluntary drinking, eating, posture, breathing and stool passage are appropriate while seeking any available remote or in-person veterinary assistance.
