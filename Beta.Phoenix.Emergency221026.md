This video documents a health incident involving Beta Phoenix on 22 October 2026. Before recording, I had observed very slight tail bobbing. Her feathers were not fluffed. I had already observed enough to judge that she needed to be checked and acted accordingly.

During the recorded episode, Beta extended her neck outward, opened her beak and made a loud, strained respiratory noise. Her breathing appeared to clear temporarily, and the gasping sound ceased. She subsequently jumped from the garden table and emitted a very short, high-pitched noise immediately after landing. The significance of this final vocalisation remains undetermined.

Recording and intervention context: I began recording intentionally for analysis and recordkeeping. My decision to check and assist Beta was based on observations made before and during recording; I stopped recording to assist her. The recording should not be interpreted as evidence of delayed intervention or negligence.

A previous Silkie hen died within seconds during an incident that appeared similar to me. That experience makes this event particularly concerning, but visual similarity alone does not establish that the incidents had the same cause.

This is a contemporaneous observational record, not a veterinary diagnosis. The cause of the respiratory episode, the significance of the post-landing vocalisation and any relationship between the two remain unconfirmed. The visual impression that Beta's plumage appears laced is descriptive only and is not a confirmed breed identification.

Recordkeeping principle: Preserve the observation, chronology and uncertainty. Do not infer a diagnosis or outcome beyond the available evidence.
