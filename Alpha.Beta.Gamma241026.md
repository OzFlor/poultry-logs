# GameSilkies Breeding Decisions: Docility, Sentinel Behaviour, Health, Appearance and Genetic Diversity

I would appreciate advice from experienced chicken breeders, especially those with experience in crossbreeding, selecting for temperament, and managing genetic diversity in a small breeding population.

I am documenting an ongoing experimental cross between Gamefowl and Silkies, which I call **GameSilkies**. This is my own developing breeding project, not an established breed. The intended breeding direction is Gamefowl hens × Silkie roosters.

I currently have three F1 birds that present an interesting selection dilemma. Their differences in temperament, behaviour, health history and appearance are making me reconsider which birds should contribute to the next generation.

I would like to hear how other breeders would approach this decision, including what they would breed, what they would avoid, and what additional information they would want before deciding.

## 1. The three F1 birds

*Weights below are the latest recorded on 23 October 2026, not measurements taken today. Morning weights are recorded with the crop state noted; sleeping weights include the effect of crop contents.*

### Alpha Phoenix — Gamy3

* **Hatched:** 13 June 2026
* **Morning weight, 23 October:** 746 g, empty crop.
* **Sleeping weight, 23 October:** 799 g, very full crop.

**Temperament and behaviour:**

Alpha is closer to my original goal of producing manageable birds suitable for a domestic garden environment. However, I would like to understand whether his comparatively passive response to potential predators should influence breeding selection.

### Beta Phoenix — Gamy4

* **Hatched:** 14 June 2026

* **Parentage:** Same mother as Alpha Phoenix. Almost certainly the same father, although parentage has not been DNA-confirmed.

**Temperament and behaviour:**

### Gamma Phoenix — Gamy5

* **Hatched:** 10 July 2026
* **Morning weight, 23 October:** 874 g, empty crop.
* **Sleeping weight, 23 October:** 941 g, very full crop.
* **Parentage:** Different mother from Alpha and Beta. He may share their father, but this is unconfirmed.
* **Maternal line:** GameSilkies BS gene-mother line.

**Temperament and behaviour:**

* Very docile towards me as caretaker.
* More aggressive towards other birds.
* Gives a very loud alarm call when he notices a potential predator.
* Has shown a strong sentinel response during recorded encounters with a cat.
* Appears more alert and willing to confront situations than Alpha and Beta in the encounters I have observed.

Gamma is also considerably more elegant and handsome to my eye. His appearance is an important consideration for me, although I do not want to select a bird on looks alone.

His aggression towards other birds is a genuine concern for my intended purpose. I want birds that can live with others without creating excessive conflict, even if a strong sentinel response could be useful.

## 2. Gamma's health history: resilience or an unresolved question?

Gamma has had a more concerning health history than I would prefer in a future breeding candidate.

His records include:

* Repeated observations of stools containing substantial blood.
* Suspected coccidiosis, which I have not established as a confirmed diagnosis.
* Direct observation of live, moving parasite segments consistent with tapeworm proglottids.
* Deworming and continued monitoring.
* Continued weight gain and relatively little observed lethargy during periods when these health concerns were present.

On 23 October, Gamma weighed 874 g in the morning and 941 g at sleeping time, when his crop was very full. His morning weight was 41 g higher than the previous morning's recorded weight.

Despite the health concerns, he has continued to show activity and strong alarm behaviour. I find this noteworthy, but I recognise that it does not prove he has a stronger immune system or genetically superior disease resistance.

I would like to know how experienced breeders would evaluate this history. Would you consider it relevant to breeding selection, or would you require a much longer period of health monitoring and better-controlled evidence before drawing any conclusions?

I would not want to propagate a health problem simply because a bird appeared to cope with it. Equally, I would not want to discard a potentially valuable bird based on an incomplete understanding of what happened.

## 3. My breeding priorities are changing

My original objective is to develop compact, attractive birds that are manageable around people and suitable for a secure domestic garden environment.

My priorities include:

1. Docility towards people.
2. Compatibility with other chickens.
3. Good general health and sustainable growth.
4. Attractive appearance and conformation.
5. Useful awareness of potential predators, without excessive aggression towards flockmates.
6. Maintaining genetic diversity as the project develops.

Alpha and Beta currently fit the first two priorities better than Gamma.

Gamma, however, offers a different combination of characteristics: strong predator-alert behaviour, a more confrontational response to other birds, and an appearance I particularly like.

I am now seriously considering whether **Beta and Gamma might both be valuable parents for the next generation**, rather than concentrating selection on Alpha and Beta because they are the more docile pair.

This could give me more breeding options and help avoid narrowing the project around one maternal line. However, I do not yet know whether Gamma and Beta share a father, and I understand that this uncertainty matters when planning matings.

I would appreciate advice on how to balance these considerations without compromising my goal of producing manageable birds.
