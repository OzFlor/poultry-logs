Reading this record

- Observation: What was seen, measured, fed, or recorded.
- Interpretation: A possible meaning of an observation, not a confirmed diagnosis.
- Hypothesis: A possible explanation requiring further evidence.
- Weight change: Percentages are calculated from the inferred previous morning/reference weight when the current weight and recorded 24-hour change are available.
- Crop status: Morning and sleeping weights are separate observations. Crop contents can materially affect weight.
- Mix totals: These summarize the individual feeding entries recorded in this log. They are not treatment recommendations.
- Stool attribution: Shared-area stools may not be attributable to a specific bird.
- Time notation: "ts" denotes a timestamp; "lt" denotes logging time. Neither should automatically be treated as the exact time an event occurred.

Afternoon

- 13:25 (timestamp): Formed green stool with orange material the author identified, with high certainty based on repeated prior observations, as encapsulated blood. A substantial white urate cap was present.
- 14:53 — original stool photograph: The author identifies the orange material as blood with high certainty based on its similarity to material observed in many of Gamma's previous stools. This is the author's longitudinal identification, not merely an inference from colour in this single image.
  - White material: Visually thick and extensive; exact composition remains unconfirmed.
  - Additional structure: A small, rice-grain-like, rubbery structure was found after the photograph. Its identity remains undetermined.
  - Overall appearance: The author considered Gamma to look better despite the continuing blood observation.

Interpretation limits

The log records variable stool colours and consistencies, mucus, white material, and recurrent orange/red components. The author's high-certainty identification of blood draws on repeated observations across multiple days. Nevertheless, the record does not establish the underlying cause.

A stool without visible blood does not prove that bleeding has stopped. Pigmentation from food or herbal tea may complicate visual assessment, but the available evidence does not establish that either caused the observed pattern.

The white rubber-like structures are not confirmed to be intestinal lining, parasites, or any other specific material.

The author's impression that Gamma looked better is preserved as an observation of overall appearance, not a declaration of recovery. Given the repeated prior blood observations and today's reported recurrence, the condition remains unresolved in this record. Veterinary assessment is important if available.
