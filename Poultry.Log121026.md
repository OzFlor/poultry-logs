7. General observations — caretaker and recording conditions

Weighing procedure and limitations

The caretaker performs morning weighing while it is still dark, using combinations of bedroom, bathroom and kitchen lighting. Doors are opened or closed to adjust the available light while keeping the birds calm and preventing them from moving freely around the house.

Birds are picked up individually, weighed, and recorded on paper. The caretaker calculates weight differences as soon as possible because the results can inform immediate decisions, including whether to provide Mix1 or isolate a bird.

This procedure involves multiple simultaneous tasks:

- Handling and identifying individual birds.
- Reading the scale and recording measurements.
- Calculating weight changes.
- Adjusting lighting and moving papers to a brighter location.
- Managing aggression and other flock behaviour.
- Preparing supplemental feed and isolating individual birds when necessary.

Some birds cannot be identified easily in dim light, so the caretaker sometimes uses a phone light. The entire procedure takes approximately 20 minutes or longer. Branquinho and Pipi are particularly difficult to place on the scale.

The caretaker acknowledges that dim lighting, fatigue and multitasking can cause reading or calculation errors. Figures are checked later when possible. The records represent working observations subject to explicit correction; uncertainty must not be silently removed.

Drainage observations

The rainwater drainers remained blocked. Images recorded on the previous day showed a drainer filled to the level of the concrete top base.

A hole in the garden contained an underground water level below the level of the drainer. The hydraulic relationship between these locations has not been established.

Recordkeeping and interpretation note — 14:52 LT

The caretaker noted concern that some AI-generated interpretations of the logs had introduced medical cautions without adequately acknowledging the integrity and limitations of the underlying records.

The caretaker does not claim to provide veterinary diagnoses. When urgent care is needed and the cause is unknown, interventions may be based on prior experience, empirical observations or trial and error. The records should preserve what was observed, what action was taken, and what remains unknown.

For example, the sequence of visible worm-like material after praziquantel, persistent bleeding, subsequent treatment for coccidiosis and cessation of bleeding on approximately day three or four is a chronology of observations and interventions. Whether Branquinho also had coccidiosis, and whether the treatment caused the improvement, remain unresolved questions.

Recordkeeping principle: An explicit statement that the cause is unknown is a valid record of uncertainty, not a claim that a diagnosis has been established.
