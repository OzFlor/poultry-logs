- Persistent eye closure, worsening mobility, and inability to handle or feed him safely warrant veterinary assessment if it can be accessed.

The original observations should be retained for continuity, including the distinction between food items, measured water, independent drinking, and procedures that could not be completed.

11.1 Blood and mucus in stools

Visible blood and mucus appeared reduced compared with the previous day, but abnormal stools persisted in several areas. Some stools had high-confidence visual signs of blood, while many could not be assigned to individual birds.

Priority: Continue preserving the stool sequence and uncertainty of attribution. The apparent reduction should not be treated as resolution while blood or substantial mucus continues.

11.2 Aggression and flock safety

Repeated attacks involving Pequenina, Pipi, Branquinho, and Gamma Phoenix contributed to a disruptive day. Branquinho's inability to balance reliably makes his attacks especially concerning for both himself and other birds.

The chicks were attacked, and Blue Neck was also observed attacking other chicks and the mother. Separation and controlled access remain important while the behaviour persists.

The evening disturbance led to birds leaving their usual locations, with some reportedly reaching the street. Missing birds, outdoor exposure, and movement over freshly poured concrete added further management risks.

The caretaker reported worsening function, severe agitation, an increasingly difficult feeding process, persistent closure of the left eye, and a shift in attention from leg therapy back to the neck.

The record supports concern about both his welfare and the risks his behaviour creates for the flock.
