2. GAMMA PHOENIX — AGGRESSION AND MANAGEMENT

1 November 2026 — Continued behavioural incidents

The previous log recorded Gamma attacking and dominating the Red Blue Neck mother, requiring immediate intervention and isolation.

The current log records further aggression involving the broody hens and other birds.

2 November 2026 — 11:04 and 11:05

Two separate fighting incidents were recorded:

- 11:04: Gamma Phoenix fighting with Red Neck. The observer interrupted the feeding activity to stop the fight.
- 11:05: Gamma Phoenix fighting with Red Blue Neck. The observer again interrupted the feeding activity to stop the fight.

The observer reported that the Gamefowl birds and Gamma were becoming increasingly aggressive. The mothers of the chicks had been provoking Gamma while protecting their chicks, and the interactions appeared to be escalating beyond manageable levels.

1 November 2026 — 14:02: Multiple mounting and aggression incidents

The previous log recorded a chaotic sequence involving Gamma, Patrícia, Black Neck and Black Red Neck.

The relevant observations are retained here because they document Gamma's behaviour and its consequences for flock management:

1. Gamma mounted Patrícia after she had been rescued from an earlier attack by Pequenina.
2. Patrícia became distressed, with rapid breathing and tail bobbing. The observer intervened and moved her to safety.
3. Gamma subsequently pursued the two broody hens.
4. Gamma mounted Black Red Neck on top of the movable coop in area 0.
5. After intervention, Gamma pursued Black Neck and then returned to chase Black Red Neck into the garden.
6. The observer had to intervene repeatedly to prevent further mounting and pursuit.

The observer described Gamma as extremely fast, persistent and difficult to control during these incidents.

Important qualification: The observer believed Gamma may have discharged semen during one mounting incident, but this was not confirmed.

Behavioural interpretation

The observations across 1–2 November document repeated aggression, pursuit and mounting behaviour involving several hens.

This is relevant to the project's objectives because manageability, compatibility with other birds and suitability for a docile ornamental/pet-oriented direction are important considerations.

The incidents do not establish that these behaviours are genetically inherited or representative of all GameSilkies. They document Gamma's observed behaviour under the conditions recorded.

5. PROJECT ASSESSMENT — CURRENT ENTRY

The observations from this log highlight three areas requiring continued documentation:

1. Gamma's behavioural management

Repeated aggression and mounting incidents involving several hens were recorded across 1–2 November. These events raise practical concerns about compatibility and manageability. They should remain part of Gamma's individual behavioural history.

2. F0 chick health monitoring

Arriba showed signs that concerned the observer, including subtle behavioural changes, a soft and relatively empty crop, and an abnormal-looking small stool. Black was also considered unwell later in the day. The cause remains undetermined.

3. Continued individual growth records

All three F0 chicks and all three F1 GameSilkies recorded weight increases during the stated comparison period. The reliability limitations of weighing small, moving chicks must remain attached to the F0 measurements.

Evidence limitations

This assessment is based only on the observations supplied for the period 1–2 November 2026. It does not represent a review of the complete historical GameSilkies archive, earlier raw logs, all imagery, or all supporting protocols.

No genetic, diagnostic or inherited behavioural conclusions are made.
